# Krista's teaching: The Machine That Turns Authority into Clients

## The one big idea
A live event, where you teach and the teaching does the selling, is the machine that turns all the authority you have built into people on your calendar. It collapses months of trust-building into one sitting, and it works at any size, from a library room to a Zoom link. The event is only one of ten steps; the machine built around the hour is what matters.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **FIRST Market Leader System (the S and the T)**: this covers two letters of it. Scaled means you do the work of one appointment and reach a whole room. Top Choice means that while someone learns from you for an hour, they quietly decide who they will hire, and no competitor gets a turn.
- **Two rooms, one machine**: an in-person event and an online event are the same machine at two scales. In person wins on proximity. Online wins on reach, comfort and being less invasive (people can check you out before they are ready to be seen), and it records itself.
- **Serve, not sell**: the event has to be genuinely useful. The ask is one small piece at the end; everything before it is a gift.
- **Name the event after what the attendee gets**: never name it after the format (never "my seminar"). Name it after the benefit, in the place where they live. This pre-frames you as advisor, makes it specific, gives the event a reason to exist, and makes the ask legitimate.
- **The Success Levers**: Registration, Show Up, Stay Rate, Eyes on the Ask, Take Action, Conversion, and Follow Up. Each is a separate number you can fix on its own.
- **ILCO (Irresistible Life-Changing Opportunity)**: the moment you stop teaching and invite the room to take one step. For a service pro, it is a private session.
- **Seeding three beliefs**: this choice matters; belief in the vehicle (your specific approach); belief in you.
- **EPIC in the room**: Equity (open on what they have on the line), Presentation, Impact, Campaign (an event is the middle of a campaign, not a night).
- **Informational buyer vs. emotional buyer**: Krista renames the "logical" buyer "informational" because they simply need more information. Your job is to inform them, not wait them out.
- **Authority Funnel Stack**: a sequence you run, not a list of things you own.
- **Community Market Leader**: what this looks like on a Tuesday night.

## The steps or process she teaches
The ten steps to fill the room and run the machine:
1. Run educational ads to the right people, aimed only at getting a raised hand for the topic.
2. Send them to a page with exactly one job: registration (no nav bar, no exits).
3. Keep the form simple: name, email, phone.
4. Send them to a confirmation page with a thirty-second video of you.
5. and 6. Texts and emails: instant confirmation, a short sequence with a testimonial and a two-minute video, and reminders at 24 hours, 1 hour and 10 minutes.
7. Call them to confirm attendance (can be automated or your front desk).
8. Host Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday at 7 p.m.
9. Make one offer at the end: a private session.
10. Book it before they cool off, within minutes, then make the real offer in that session.

The four ILCO rules: serve, don't sell (about ninety percent value, ten percent offer); your best content goes right before the ask; make one ask; seed the whole way through.

Other ways to fill the room: invite past clients, put a QR code for the next event on everything, have a co-marketing partner invite their list, list in-person events at the library, community center or chamber, offer to host at a big local employer through HR.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- More than 72 three-day live virtual events and more than 200 webinars (per Krista).
- Roughly 70% informational buyers and 30% emotional (Krista's own working figures, not external studies).
- A 10% show-up benchmark for a free virtual event (Krista's own working figure).
- Three days converts much higher than two (per Krista).
- Some of her students attended three, four or even five events before enrolling (per Krista).
- A student's 75 registered and 42 showed, a 56% show rate; more than 1,500 people through her events in nine months, one a week (from Krista's own records of the student's events).

## Her stories and examples
- Example events: a dentist on sleep apnea, an estate attorney on dying without a will, an advisor on an old 401k. Lesson: rename each around the attendee's problem.
- Two people showed up to one event and both were ideal: specific beats big.
- Danielle, a loan officer, had two people at her first event and left with two applications.
- A busy student who said "I'm too busy" ran events weekly and built them into a main pillar.
- Krista paid to produce and ship her book to attendees before events, and her conversion rose.
- If nobody shows, present anyway and record it: the recording goes to every no-show.

## What to do next
Pick one specific person with one specific problem and name them in the title. Build the ten-step machine. Run a small first event, record it, change one lever, and run it again. Send your book before and your recording after. Her teaching on the sale before the sale turns this into a recorded video (the VSL).
