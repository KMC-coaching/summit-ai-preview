# Krista's teaching: Why being known matters

## The one big idea
Krista argues there are two ways a business goes: be known, or go broke. The people a community knows get chosen, and the ones it does not know wait for a phone that rings less every year. Her one-line proof is "Top choice equals top marketer": the person who wins is not the most skilled or hardest working, but the best known, and getting known is a marketing skill anyone can learn.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **Be known, or go broke.** The title and the core argument. In her words it is arithmetic, not a scare line. Communities choose the names they already know.
- **Top choice equals top marketer.** The sentence her whole teaching is built to prove. To be the one people pick, you have to become the best marketer in your field, not the most skilled person in the room.
- **Tactics versus strategy.** A tactic is a specific thing you do (short form video, Facebook ads, a class for your community, getting found in AI search). Tactics change constantly and have a shelf life. Strategy sits underneath and never changes: be known before you are needed, win before you arrive, become the obvious authority everybody in your community already thinks of.
- **The two layers of her teaching.** Strategy lessons are the foundation and never expire. Tactic lessons show how to express that strategy with the tools available now, so you can swap in a new tool and aim it at the same permanent goal.
- **The quiet comparison.** In many professions nobody interviews three providers. The comparison happens silently, in someone's head, in about nine seconds, and you never learn your name was in it. Krista says that is worse than losing a visible bid, because you cannot fix a loss you never saw.

## The steps or process she teaches
The Introduction does not give a step-by-step process. It gives a reading approach:
1. Treat the strategy lessons like a foundation.
2. Treat the tactic lessons as how you express that strategy with the tools you have.
3. Take what you need, put it to work, and do not wait until you feel ready, because you will never feel ready.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- Per Krista, she sold 69 homes her first year in the business, brand new, outselling people who had been at it twenty years.
- Per Krista, every framework she teaches is something she did herself at the highest level, or something her students do in their own businesses. Every number is real and every named person is real. She says nothing she teaches is invented.
- Per Krista, almost none of her approach (direct response marketing, sales funnels, authority positioning, video sales letters) was taught in her industry. She took it from the best marketers outside her business, which is why nobody around her could be compared to her.
- She says most marketing books have a shelf life of about a year and a half because they only teach tactics.

## Her stories and examples
- **The lowest point.** Krista starts with the lowest point of her early career. She decided then that she would build a business that did not depend on anyone else, and that decision is why she teaches this. Lesson: a lot can change in a single year, and it starts the moment you decide to begin.
- **The three-firm family and the nine-second comparison.** Some people lose in the open (a bid goes to another company, a family interviews three firms). Most never see the race at all (nobody interviews three dentists or collects quotes from chiropractors). Lesson: the fix is the same either way, which is to be the known name before the comparison happens.
- **Skilled but invisible.** She describes people better at the work than the busiest person in town whom nobody knows exists, charging half of what they are worth, one slow quarter from panic. Lesson: that is not bad luck or a bad economy, it is a trap almost everyone in the business is caught in.

## What to do next
- Accept the premise that being good at the work stopped being enough, and that marketing is a learnable skill like the work itself.
- Notice the difference between a tactic you use today and the strategy underneath it, so you are not relearning your business every couple of years.
- Keep the examples in mind as they move between a dentist, a contractor, an attorney and a med spa: if an example is not your field, the principle still is.
- Start now rather than waiting to feel ready, and move on to her teaching on the commodity trap, where she names the trap.
