# Krista's teaching: Win Before You Arrive

## The one big idea
You do not win a client in the meeting room. You win weeks earlier, during the research phase, when a person is quietly deciding who to call. Krista splits that into two halves: being known before you are needed (the broad half, aimed at a whole market) and winning before you arrive (the narrow half, which starts the moment one specific person raises a hand and ends when you sit down with them). Krista says the first half fills the room and the second half wins the person, and the second half is where almost everyone leaves money on the table.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **Known before you're needed**: the broad half. Everything built so far (videos, ads, answers an AI can find) going out to a whole market for months, aimed at a place rather than a person.
- **Winning before you arrive**: the narrow half. The window, usually a few days, between one person raising a hand and you sitting down with them. It has a name, a face and a deadline.
- **FIRST Market Leader System, the T (Top Choice)**: Found, Impossible to Compare, Relevant, Scaled, Top Choice. Top Choice means that when someone is ready to hire, you are already the name they know, remember and prefer. Krista also ties the R (Relevant) to the 7-11-4 content.
- **Zero Moment of Truth**: Google's name for the stretch where a buyer decides alone, long before talking to a salesperson. The decision gets made during research, not the conversation.
- **Pre-frame / Halo Effect**: a first impression planted ahead of time colors everything after it. You can set the frame on purpose with hours of useful content before you meet.
- **Parasocial Relationship**: the one-sided feeling of knowing someone you have only watched. When it turns two-sided at the handshake, the meeting feels like a reunion and the appointment becomes a confirmation.
- **The delete filter**: the brain deletes nearly everything. Four things get past it: high stakes, a pattern break, free value, and familiarity (repeated exposure in more than one place), which Krista calls the strongest and most skipped.
- **7-11-4**: seven hours of engagement, eleven touch points, four platforms. Krista is explicit that it is a rule of thumb and a memorable target, not a study.
- **Visible and familiar beats viral**: the goal is the few hundred people who could hire you, not a million strangers.
- **Reciprocity**: when someone gives us something first, we feel a pull to give back.
- **Premier Listing System**: Krista's real estate version of the full pre-appointment package, one digital half and one physical half.

## The steps or process she teaches
The three things to send when someone raises a hand:
1. Your actual plan for them, in writing: one page, their name at the top, what you would do first, second and third and what each step protects them from.
2. The answer to the question they are too embarrassed to ask (usually about money or being taken advantage of), written out before they have to ask it.
3. A sixty-second video from you, made on your phone, saying their name, what will happen, how long it takes and the one thing to have ready.

What never goes in: no price (not a number, range or "starting at"), no diagnosis, no contract.

Timing: the moment they raise a hand, same day or next day, always before the appointment, never at it.

Delivery: do not just email it. Deliver something physical (porch, mailbox, hands). If you are too busy, hire a runner; if you truly cannot, email the smaller version and go build the bigger one.

The compounding journey Krista sketches over about four months: eleven touches before she speaks to you (videos, retargeting, a single-purpose page, the guide, emails, monthly update, a postcard, an AI answer with your name, a community sighting, a workshop), then five touches after she raises a hand (confirmation, package, persuasion video, proof, day-before reminder).

## Numbers and claims she makes
- Only about 3% of the market needs you today (per Krista).
- Shoppers doubled their sources before deciding, from 5.2 to 10.4, and 84% of buyers use online research (Google and Shopper Sciences, Zero Moment of Truth study, 2011).
- 7-11-4 is a marketing rule of thumb, not a study; the 10.4 sources figure is the checkable anchor.
- Warm/cold lecturer experiment: one swapped word changed ratings of the same lecture (Harold Kelley, 1950).
- Dunbar: roughly 150 people known well, around 1,500 recognizable.
- Restaurant candy study (Strohmetz and colleagues, 2002): no candy about 15% tip, one candy almost 18%, a second candy offered with a personal touch roughly 21% higher.
- BYU sampling research: the lift lasts weeks and beats end-of-aisle displays.
- Per Krista: she won the listing 93 to 97 percent of the time when she sat at a kitchen table, and was in the top one percent in her county and nationally, with this package as the mechanism.
- The "twenty, fifty, seventy-five, a thousand percent" ladder is playful, not a real statistic, by her own admission.

## Her stories and examples
- Two advisors, same family: one they had watched for hours and received a package from, one a cold stranger with a folder. The family decided before either spoke.
- Her closing line to clients: tell them to ask other candidates for a plan in writing, a course and authorship. She never names competitors; she hands the client a measuring stick.
- Gregory: his client was heading to a competitor with a relative in the business. He shared one teaching video ("Thinking About Buying") and Lisa and Clint came back asking him to represent them.

## What to do next
- Write down every touch point you currently have, including weak ones. Most people reach three. That number is your real plan; the rest of her teaching aims at eleven.
- Build the three-part package once; personalize only the name, plan and 60-second video.
- Pick three or four platforms and let repurposing carry one recorded video across them.
- Look at everything else through one question: does this get me known before I am needed?
