# Training: The Power of Video and Why to Use It (Krista Mashore training)

## The one big idea
Video builds know, like and trust faster than anything else, and most local professionals still are not using it consistently, which is a big opening for you. The reason businesses fail is rarely a bad service; it is that they do not show up consistently and their audience forgets them. Krista says to stop waiting, record on your phone, and choose progress over perfection.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
**Know, like and trust:** people want to work with people they know, like and trust, and video gets them there faster.

**Parasocial relationship:** the one-sided relationship a viewer builds with you by watching your content, before you ever meet.

**Views while you snooze:** video works for you around the clock, and people can binge it.

**Winning before you arrive:** when someone thinks about what you do, they already think of you before they need the service.

**One to many marketing:** connection at scale (she says another training covers this).

**Perception is reality:** the more you show up, the more people perceive you as an expert and authority.

**Progress, not perfection / perfectly imperfect / done is better than perfect:** people respond to connection and authenticity more than polish.

## The steps or process she teaches
Her practical setup advice:
1. Use your cell phone; no fancy gear needed.
2. Check the sound works when you first hit record.
3. Make sure nothing odd or distracting is in the background and no noise (such as a passing train).
4. Stand in front of a window for light, not with the window behind you.
5. Speak to your audience and serve them, then go.
Homework:
1. Write down your biggest fear about video.
2. List ten topics your ideal dream client would care about.
3. Record a video and post it, with no script.

## Numbers and claims she makes
These are stated by Krista as statistics in the training; treat them as her claims, not verified facts.
- 91% of businesses use video as a marketing tool, but only 30% of small local businesses use it consistently, and she says professionals are likely fewer still.
- Only 22% of service businesses post video weekly.
- Only 16% of small local businesses use paid social ads effectively or consistently, and 70% of small business owners say they do not fully understand how to run ads or track results.
- 85% of consumers want more video from brands.
- 95% of a message is retained from video versus 10% from text.
- Video is 53 times more likely to rank on page one of Google.
- A video on a landing page can boost conversions by 80%.
- Video is shared 1,200% more than text and images combined, and the brain processes it 60,000 times faster than text (a loose spoken figure).
- She also says one minute of video equals 1.8 million words (a loose spoken claim, not a verified fact).

## Her stories and examples
- **Students who were mocked for doing video.** Family and others made fun of them, and then they began taking over their community and profession. The lesson: do not let judgment stop you.
- **Her own nerves.** She admits she worries about video too. The lesson: everyone does, so go anyway.
- **Before and after of a real estate agent.** She shows the agent's first video and one after working with her. The lesson: you can improve quickly with practice.
- **Meeting clients in person.** You cannot put a bag over your head or pause 87 times, so just be yourself on video too.

## What to do next
- Write down your fear, list ten dream client topics, and record an unscripted video.
- Start with Facebook and Instagram, with paid ads to make sure the video is seen.
- Show your work (before and afters, meetings with clients) if your business is visual.
