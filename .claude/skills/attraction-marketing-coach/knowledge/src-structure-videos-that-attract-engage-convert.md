# Training: How to Structure Videos That Attract, Engage and Convert (Krista Mashore training)

## The one big idea
Every video should follow one simple structure: hook, align with their feelings, show your authority, teach, then give a call to action. The most important part is the hook, because you have only seconds to get attention, and the more attention you keep, the more you convert. Match the style of the video to the channel it is on.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
**The video structure:** hook, align with emotions, establish credibility, teach and serve, call to action.

**The hook:** the opening that speaks directly to a pain point, makes a bold statement or a big promise, and tells them what they will learn. It is not "Hi, I'm [name]."

**Align with emotions:** let people know you get how they feel, that it is not their fault, and that they are not alone.

**Credibility (not bragging):** social proof such as years of experience and specific outcomes. She says to avoid claiming "I'm the best" or "number one."

**Searchable video:** use titles and hooks that match what your client would type into Google or YouTube, with the title and first spoken line matching.

**Logic and emotion:** great videos speak to both. Logic is steps and tips; emotion is fear, frustration, relief and confidence.

**Tease, a taste, not the whole buffet:** leave them wanting more.

**Pattern interrupt / interruption-based marketing:** paid ads that interrupt people scrolling, used to build brand awareness on Facebook so the algorithm learns who likes you and you can build retargeting audiences.

## The steps or process she teaches
1. Hook them instantly with a pain point, bold statement or big promise.
2. Align with their emotions and validate what they struggle with.
3. Establish credibility so they keep listening.
4. Teach and serve with one actionable takeaway, simple and clear, in steps.
5. Tease what is next to make them curious.
6. Give a specific call to action matched to the purpose of the video.
Her pre-record checklist: hook under five seconds (aim for under three), the emotion is clear, credibility is stated, the value is actionable, and the call to action is simple and direct.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- Per Krista, you have less than three seconds to capture attention, and she says it is closer to 1.7 seconds now (a loose spoken claim, not a verified fact).
- Her credibility examples (filing over 11,000 returns, closing 100-plus homes a year) are sample lines to show the style, not a claim that these are a particular person's numbers.
- She says a mentor taught her that if you are not afraid you are giving away too much, you are not giving away enough.

## Her stories and examples
- **Hook examples across industries.** Tired of overpaying the IRS; selling your home without an agent, here is what you need to know first; sick of hiding your smile. The lesson: each hook speaks directly to one person's pain.
- **Tease examples.** "That's just one way to save on taxes, I've got two more." The lesson: give one tip and promise more.
- **Call to action examples.** Download the free guide, sign up for the workshop, book a discovery call, watch the next video, or DM a word for a checklist. The lesson: pick one that fits the video; do not spend the whole video begging for likes and subscribes.
- **YouTube versus Facebook.** On YouTube people are comparing and learning, so longer videos work. On Facebook, a long first-exposure video is the wrong fit; make ads feel like a friend sharing something useful, and on Instagram stories make an ad look like a story. The lesson: match style to the channel.

## What to do next
- Print out or write down the video format.
- Make your next set of videos use it: short hook, clear emotion, stated credibility, actionable value, simple call to action.
- Build titles and hooks around what your client would search.
- Pick horizontal or vertical based on the platform.
- Do not overthink it; done is better than perfect, but always write a very strong hook, on videos, emails and headlines alike.
