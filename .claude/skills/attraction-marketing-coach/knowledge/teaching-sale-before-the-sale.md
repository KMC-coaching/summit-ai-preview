# Krista's teaching: The Sale Before the Sale

## The one big idea
Record one ten-to-fifteen-minute video, a VSL (video sales letter), built in a deliberate order, that explains how you work and why it is not the same as everyone else. Send it ahead of you to everyone who raises a hand, so you win before you arrive. Almost nobody in service industries uses one, so the slot is open.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **VSL (video sales letter)**: a structured, recorded piece of persuasion that does the work of a whole sales conversation without you in the room. It is different from the sixty-second hello video from her teaching on winning before you arrive, which is just the handshake.
- **FIRST Market Leader System (S and I)**: Scaled (build once, works on everyone) and Impossible to Compare (nobody else has one, and what is inside it).
- **Two ways it pays off**: for businesses that get compared out loud (bids, quotes, interviews), it pre-sells the meeting; for the silent comparison, it removes the comparison before it forms.
- **Mechanism**: a method you use that your competition cannot even describe, let alone run. "Family owned" and "we care" are not mechanisms. A named process is something a person can hold; an unnamed one is a vibe.
- **Data that contradicts the default**: name what everyone does, show one real number that undercuts it, say the source, then stop talking.
- **Show the gap**: when you have no number, show the standard way and your way side by side on screen. A demonstration cannot be fact-checked wrong.
- **Commodity Trap**: a beautiful but generic video is the trap in a nicer outfit.
- **One video, many openings**: the middle is the same for everyone; four spots swap by viewer type.
- **EPIC on video**: Equity (open on what they have on the line), Presentation, Impact, Campaign (the ads and funnel that carry the video).

## The steps or process she teaches
Four steps for data: 1. Name the standard approach calmly. 2. Give one real number that undercuts it. 3. Say the source out loud by name. 4. Stop and let them conclude.

Where to find a number: your industry association, government agencies, peer-reviewed journals, and your own records (outcomes, timelines, reviews).

The eight sections, in order:
1. The hook (first fifteen seconds, one specific person in one situation).
2. The stakes and the promise.
3. A short credibility line.
4. The gap (two or three minutes).
5. The mechanism (the heart).
6. The proof (on screen while you talk).
7. The authority stack (after they have seen the value).
8. What happens next (no hard pitch, plus your mission in one sentence).

Eight viewer versions, swapped at four spots (hook, after the gap, after the proof, one comparison beat near the close): the first timer, the second opinion, the one who got burned, the one who has been putting it off, the emergency, the referral, the price shopper, and standard.

How to build: write it before recording; time a page read aloud (about 140 words a minute, so about 1,700 words for twelve minutes); use a teleprompter; record the middle once, then the hooks back to back, in the same shirt, spot and light; show things on screen; improve it by sending it to twenty people; rebuild about once a year. Put the staff member who will serve the client in the video too.

## Her stories and examples
- Krista built hers as a busy agent tired of repeating the same appointment presentation; she stopped having to prove herself in the room.
- Remodeler, dentist and advisor mechanism examples show specificity as proof.
- Linda showed her plan instead of telling it and won. Darren landed more work once he had marketing assets to show.

## What to do next
Build your mechanism if you do not have one, find a defensible number or a visual gap, write the eight sections, record, and place the video before consultations, on ad landing pages, in guide follow-ups, with workshop registrants, and to people who asked about price and went quiet. Her teaching on being the Ritz covers keeping clients.
