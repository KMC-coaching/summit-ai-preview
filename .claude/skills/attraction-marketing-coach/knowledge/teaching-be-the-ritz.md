# Krista's teaching: Be the Ritz

## The one big idea
Getting chosen is only the beginning. What happens after someone says yes decides whether you still have a business in five years. Top Choice is not a moment you win but a position you hold, by delivering far past the average and by staying in your clients' minds so they refer, return and rehire you.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **FIRST Market Leader System (the T)**: Top Choice. Her teaching on winning before you arrive showed how someone becomes your client before you meet; this is how you stay their only choice afterward.
- **Fulfillment**: the stage where you are actually delivering. Krista says getting it wrong ends a business faster than any other mistake, because an ignored or confused client tells people and the referrals from them are gone.
- **Marketing plus service**: great marketing with average service is a bucket with a hole in it; great service with no marketing makes you the best-kept secret in town. You need both.
- **Be the Ritz, not the Motel 6**: the Motel 6 version is competent, clean and forgettable. The Ritz is service so far past average that people feel the difference before they can name it. The guiding question is "what more can I do?" not "what is the least I can do?"
- **A thing you do vs. a thing your business does**: a personal gesture only lasts as long as your calendar; a built-in system keeps running for everyone.
- **Toot your own horn**: tell clients what you did for them that they could not see. It is information they paid for, not bragging.
- **Refer, Retain, Resell (the Three R's)**: all three run on one condition, whether they remember you.
  - **Refer**: ask at the glowing moment, out loud, in one sentence. Referrals also come from people who never hired you, once you are the known name in your community, because they have watched your videos and feel they know you.
  - **Retain**: the relationship does not end when the work does. Life buries everyone, so stay in front of past clients. Per Krista, video does this: the same content that attracts strangers reminds past clients you exist without a "just checking in" message.
  - **Resell**: the same person will need you again as their life changes (the couple with a third child needs a bigger home, the bathroom client looks at the kitchen next). Do not assume past clients come back on their own. If someone has not heard from you in two years and has been watching someone else's videos, they call that person, because that person is the one they remember.
- **1-800-Good-Luck**: Glenn's name for handing a client off to an automated claims system.
- **Community Market Leader**: the one whose clients never went looking again and told everyone they knew.

## The steps or process she teaches
The Ritz exercise (about an hour):
1. Take a sheet of paper and write the phases of the client experience across the top: first phone call, first appointment, the day the work starts, the long middle, the finish, the month after.
2. Under each, write what happens now.
3. Write what could happen (ask what the most a human being could do at that phase).
4. The gap between the two columns is your competitive advantage.

Toot your horn habit: send regular updates (a monthly one-page note, photos behind the wall, a plain-language snapshot, a six-week status) and end each by naming the thing they could not see, in one sentence.

Refer: ask at the glowing moment with one sentence, "Can you think of someone else I could serve?"

## Her stories and examples
- Krista's coaching welcome package, chocolates around month three, sweatshirt around month five, built up one piece at a time. Her monthly personal calls broke as the company grew, while built-in pieces kept running.
- Glenn, an insurance broker, gave clients his cell number, showed up when a family's house burned, and housed them until it was rebuilt. Lesson: decide in advance what you do when a client is in trouble.
- Debbie, a loan officer, sent a client to the builder's lender to save them money, earned the agent's loyalty and ended up doing loans for whole families.
- Krista lost business for ten years by not staying in touch with past clients, and cannot rehire or refer her own tree cutter, closet builder or carpet cleaner because she forgot their names.

## What to do next
Do the Ritz sheet this week. Build at least one service piece into the business, not just into your calendar. Start telling clients what you did. Ask for referrals out loud. Keep your video content in front of past clients so they remember you. Her teaching on setting the goal covers the decision to do it.
