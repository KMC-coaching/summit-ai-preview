# Training: The Customer Acquisition Process and the Pre-Frame (Krista Mashore training)

## The one big idea
Getting someone from not knowing you to seeing you as the top-of-mind solution happens in phases: cold, warm, hot. What you do before moving someone to the next phase (the pre-frame) shapes how they think, feel and act, so you shift their state first instead of selling. Most marketers skip cold to warm and jump straight to hot, which is why acquiring customers gets expensive.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
**Pre-frame:** the intentional setup that influences how someone will think, feel or act as they move into the next phase of your sales cycle. You do not sell; you shift their state first.

**Customer acquisition phases (cold, warm, hot):** a cold customer does not know you and often does not know they have a problem. A warm customer has been exposed to you, may realize it is about them, and is searching for information and vetting you. A hot customer knows they have a problem, is actively seeking a solution, and needs to see that you are the right one.

**Bridging the gap:** moving people from where they are to where you want them using educational, informational marketing, matching your message to the phase they are in.

**Earn the right to ask:** you must build trust before asking for a name, phone number and email. The more marketing messages people see, the more you have to earn that.

**Attraction-based parasocial relationship marketing:** warming people up and changing their view of you through prior exposure.

**Tagging:** using your CRM to tag people by phase so cold contacts get one set of emails and hot contacts get another.

## The steps or process she teaches
1. Cold: hook them, earn trust, position yourself as the authority, and give a lot of value so they want to go further.
2. Warm: show up where they are looking, grab attention, and give value so they keep learning and vetting you.
3. Hot: make clear that you are the right solution for their problem.
4. Throughout, match messaging to the phase, tag contacts in your CRM, and send different workflows to different phases and different customer types (her example: one patient wanting clear aligners and another wanting cavities should not get the same message).

## Numbers and claims she makes
- Per Krista, she describes a university study where students saw the same professor presentation, but one half read a biography calling him "rather warm" and the other "rather cold." The cold group described him as self-centered, ruthless, irritable and too formal, while the warm group said they loved him. She says it involved about 30 students and that only that one word differed. This is her retelling of a study from a book she loves, not a figure to quote as verified.
- Per Krista, most marketers skip cold to warm, which makes acquisition cost outrageous and slows conversions.

## Her stories and examples
- **The "warm vs cold" professor study.** The same content landed completely differently depending on what people were told beforehand. The lesson: prior exposure and framing change perception, which is why warming people up works.
- **Dating analogy.** You would not meet someone and propose going straight to the bedroom; you text, call, coffee, lunch, dinner, and so on. The lesson: skipping steps in marketing is just as odd and costs more.
- **Veteran buyer example.** A veteran with little savings and weak credit ignores "it's a great time to buy a house" because he thinks he cannot qualify. But "Are you a veteran with not much in the bank and not the best credit? You may be able to buy a home" speaks to his real situation. The lesson: a message that names the person's situation moves them from cold to educated; then you keep adding value to take them to hot.

## What to do next
- Identify which phase your ideal client is in and write messages that match it.
- Use your avatar to shape what you say to cold audiences.
- Tag people in your CRM by phase and build separate email workflows for each.
- Do not ask cold people for their contact details before giving real value.
- Use videos short enough for cold audiences (she says a five-minute video will often get swiped past).
