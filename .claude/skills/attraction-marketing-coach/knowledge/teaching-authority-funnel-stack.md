# Krista's teaching: The Authority Funnel Stack

## The one big idea
Video, ads and AI get attention, but attention alone goes nowhere because nothing holds it. A person watches your video, finds nowhere to go next, and forgets you by Thursday; or she raises a hand and you pounce on someone who has known you eleven minutes. Krista's answer is to stop asking for the appointment and instead build one connected system, the Authority Funnel Stack, that walks a person through your teaching so they arrive already decided. Build it once and it nurtures a whole market without you.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **Don't ask for the appointment**: asking puts you in a room being compared with two others. Instead position so far ahead that people arrive decided. In place of "let's set up a meeting," you send the video, the class, the guide.
- **The Authority Funnel Stack**: five layers. Three are assets you build (video, class, book), one is the structure connecting them (the funnel), and one carries it all out to your chosen place (ads and AI).
  - **Video**: how a stranger meets you.
  - **A class you teach**: how a stranger starts trusting you; a free class or workshop of thirty or forty minutes, in a room or on a screen.
  - **A book**: why they stop comparing you to anyone.
  - **The funnel**: puts them in order and walks one person through, step by step: video to page, page to class, then the book, and the book earns the conversation.
  - **Ads and AI**: ads put what works in front of the right people repeatedly; AI writes follow-up in your words, answers late-night questions and shows who is heating up. Neither creates trust; both multiply it.
- **FIRST Market Leader System vs the Stack**: FIRST is what you become (the scoreboard). The Stack is what you build to get there (the equipment). This is the S, Scaled.
- **What a funnel actually is**: not a website, not one landing page, not an ad. A system that puts you in position as the obvious choice before anyone talks to you.
- **One funnel per question**: not one for your whole business. A funnel about "everything we do" converts almost nobody.
- **Front-door offer**: a low-priced, genuinely good first step for the person who is ready (her examples: a new patient exam and cleaning, a flat-fee will review).
- **Serve, not sell**: every touch point gives something; none says "just following up." That is the line between persistent and annoying.
- **Community Market Leader**: what one person covering a community without chasing anybody becomes.

## The steps or process she teaches
The six moving parts of a funnel:
1. The way in: an ad, video, search, AI recommendation or postcard QR code.
2. A page that does one job: picks up the exact question the video raised, no navigation, no gallery, no company history.
3. Something worth trading a name for.
4. Delivery in the next sixty seconds (late is the same as never).
5. Follow-up that runs without you.
6. The ask: one clear invitation at the end of a stretch of helping.

What is worth an email address (pick one): the number (what it costs and the three things that move the price), the checklist, the comparison (option A vs B and who each suits), the mistake list, what just changed, and the front-door offer. It must be genuinely good; the guide is an audition. The material is the twenty questions you answer out loud every week.

The sample follow-up sequence:
1. Right away: the guide plus sixty seconds of you on camera.
2. Day two: the question everyone asks after the guide.
3. Day four: a short video on a real situation, no identifying details.
4. Day seven: the thing nobody says aloud, usually money.
5. Day ten: proof, someone like them.
6. Day fourteen: the invitation.
7. Weeks three through eight: two more touches as retargeting ads to people who opened everything.
8. If nothing lands: the long list and your monthly local update, forever.

A later video that does the selling when you are not in the room belongs inside the funnel.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- Per Krista: her company policy in both businesses is a minimum of 7 to 9 touch points before expecting someone to be ready.
- Per Krista: converting a well-nurtured lead becomes "much, much easier, and almost automatic."
- About 97% of the market is not buying today (the figure she builds on throughout her teaching).
- The smallest version from her teaching on getting exposure: one video worth watching and one page to send watchers to.

## Her stories and examples
- A woman watches your video, has nowhere to go, forgets you by Thursday. Lesson: the video worked, there was nothing on the other side.
- The back-pain walkthrough: video, one-question page, guide in sixty seconds, two weeks of emails, free class, booking. She arrives as a person who already decided.
- The old retirement account example: same machine, different assets loaded in.
- Krista's own coaching business: free teaching video, then the book funnel, and only then an application, with authority built from the first thing anyone saw.
- Sarah, a coach for photographers: free tutorials, a low-priced challenge, an eight-week course, then higher coaching.
- Nina, a dance studio owner: the first parent conversation is where she learns whether expectations fit her culture. A funnel gives you room to say no.
- Carol, a financial planner: asks for financial information before the first meeting so it is spent on goals, not gathering.
- Students use the same stack for coaching, hypnosis, tapping, yoga and wellness, and a dental practice.

## What to do next
- Build one funnel around one question, get it running, then build the next.
- Choose one item from the email-address menu and make it truly useful.
- Write the six follow-up emails once, and use AI to draft them from a video transcript.
- Hand the written sequence to a front desk or assistant.
- Move on to the book and the class.
