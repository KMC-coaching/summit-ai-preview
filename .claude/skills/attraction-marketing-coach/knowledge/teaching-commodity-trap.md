# Krista's teaching: The Commodity Trap

## The one big idea
When everyone in a field looks the same, the only thing left to compare is price, and that is the Commodity Trap. Krista argues the best person in town does not win, the best known does, so the way out is to build your position by design instead of winning by default. Top choice equals top marketer, and educating the market before it needs you makes competition irrelevant.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **The Commodity Trap.** A commodity is interchangeable: one unit equals the next, so price is the only lever. Most local service businesses share the same website, the same "family owned" line, the same five-star reviews and the same pitch, so customers compare on cost.
- **Winning by default, not by design.** Getting busy through more years, hours and hustle, or through one faucet (a referral source, an insurance network, a builder, a hospital system, a handed-over book of business). Nothing about the position is protected, and effort does not transfer or compound.
- **Winning by design.** Deliberately building a position as the known, trusted, obvious expert so you get chosen on purpose, over and over.
- **Top choice equals top marketer.** To be chosen you must be the best marketer in your field. Skill matters, but skill is not what gets you chosen.
- **A business by default versus a business by design.** By default takes whatever comes (referrals, walk-ins) and cannot do it again on purpose. By design builds a position and engineers where business comes from.
- **The Larger Market Formula.** Credited to Sabri Suby (Sell Like Crazy). Only about 3% of a market needs what you do right now. About 17% are gathering information, 20% are problem aware, and 60% are not problem aware. Everyone fights over the 3% and ignores the other 97%.
- **Be known before you are needed / win before you arrive.** Educate the 97% so that by the time they raise a hand, they already know you.
- **Marketing is educating.** Krista calls it the whole game.
- **Checkers versus chess.** Checkers players react move by move. Chess players think positions. Systems beat effort and positioning beats persuasion.
- **Strategy versus tactics.** Tactics are rented and come and go (and sometimes come back). Strategy is owned. You stack new tools on old ones instead of trading them.
- **Community Market Leader®.** Her name for the known, trusted authority who gets chosen before the conversation starts. The community can be a zip code, a set of neighborhoods, or a niche served online.

## The steps or process she teaches
Krista does not lay this out as numbered steps. It gives a decision: stay a business by default, or become a business by design. Her path in short:
1. Recognize the Commodity Trap and ask why your last client really picked you.
2. Stop competing on effort and price and build a position as the obvious authority.
3. Educate the whole market (the 97%), not only the 3% who need you now.
4. Hold the strategy steady and keep upgrading and stacking tactics (old and new together).

- Per Krista, Tiffany went from about $80,000 a year on someone else's team to over a million dollars in commissions in a single year, three separate times. Another student with six months of experience took her website from 29 views to 1,800 and closed seven deals.

## Her stories and examples
- **The kitchen table with three bids.** Three contractors who look and sound the same leave the couple only price. Lesson: sameness turns you into a number.
- **Advisors, dentists, attorneys.** All say the same words, so a family sees only the fee. Lesson: the same trap sits in every profession.
- **169 homes to 12.** Krista was the busiest invisible person in her market, living on bank-owned foreclosure business. When it dried up, nobody knew her name. Lesson: when someone else controls the faucet you have an arrangement, not a business.
- **Rebuilding in two neighborhoods.** She chose to be the known name in two neighborhoods using postcards, community garage sales, and events with a Santa sled and the Easter Bunny. Then digital stacked on top. Lesson: strategy stays, tactics change, and you stack them.
- **Postcards dying and returning.** They stopped working when everyone sent them, then worked better once almost nobody did. Lesson: the tactic changed, the strategy never moved.
- **Tiffany and the six-month student.** Neither had a head start. They stopped competing on price and started being known.

## What to do next
- Answer in one clear sentence why your last client chose you.
- Ask whether you are winning by default (one faucet, one referral source) and name your own faucet.
- Decide to become a business by design and a Community Market Leader®.
- Plan to educate the whole market, and keep tactics separate from the permanent strategy.
- Get ready to claim a specific place.
