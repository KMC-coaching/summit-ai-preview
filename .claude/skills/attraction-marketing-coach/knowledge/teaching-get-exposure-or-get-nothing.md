# Krista's teaching: Get Exposure or Get Nothing

## The one big idea
Paid ads do not create authority, they distribute it. Most professionals tried the Boost button once, got nothing, and concluded ads do not work, when the real problem was no strategy and nothing waiting at the other end. Krista's argument: everybody else buys the 3% of the market that is ready now, so aim your ads at the other 97%, show them video, and retarget the people who keep watching.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **The Boost Button Is Not a Campaign**: boosting a post tells the platform only "show this to more people." It has no offer, no next step, no pixel, no retargeting and nothing behind it, so the money buys attention and throws it away.
- **The five missing pieces**: no offer, no next step, no pixel, no retargeting, and nothing behind the ad. Krista says you do not have an ads problem, you have a strategy problem that shows up on your credit card statement.
- **The Larger Market Formula (the 3%, 17%, 20%, 60% split)**: credited to Sabri Suby. About 3% need what you do right now, 17% are gathering information, 20% are problem aware, and 60% are not thinking about it. Here it becomes the guide for where to point your money.
- **Buy the 97%**: everyone else fights over the crowded 3%. The 17% and 20% (37%) are barely marketed to, cost almost nothing to reach, and will choose whoever is there while they decide.
- **FIRST Market Leader System, the F (Found)**: being visible in your chosen place to the 97% nobody else talks to.
- **Ads distribute authority**: ads carry assets (video, page, book, event) to more people. Do not run ads until you have somewhere to send people.
- **The smallest thing you can point money at**: one video answering one real question in a minute or less, plus one single-job page (picks up the video's question, offers one thing for an email, one box to fill in). Not your website, which she calls a menu.
- **Cold, Warm, Hot**: cold has never heard of you (goal: be interesting enough to be watched), warm has interacted with you (deepen it), hot raised a hand, plus past clients and people who know you.
- **Awareness ads**: ads with no call to action that keep you in front of people so they remember you when someone in their life needs you. Marketing, she says, is a reminder. Top choice equals top marketer.
- **The Pixel Is Your Basket by the Door**: a small piece of code on your page and site that records who visited and what they looked at. A student's analogy. Install it on day one, because it only collects from the moment it is on.
- **Restricted categories**: platforms treat housing, credit and lending, employment and health differently, with narrower targeting and stricter wording.
- **Retargeting Is the Whole Game**: showing your next message to people who engaged with your last one. A student calls it the gold nugget of paid advertising.

## The steps or process she teaches
The campaign, in her order:
1. Start with a problem, not an announcement (the thing keeping someone up at night).
2. Make a short series of three or four videos on that one problem, each deeper than the last.
3. Run video one to a cold, broad audience in the place you picked. Ask for nothing.
4. Retarget video two to everyone who watched video one.
5. Retarget video three to those who watched video two.
6. Send them to a page built for that one subject, not your website.
7. Let them raise a hand (guide, pricing breakdown, workshop registration).
8. Everything fires at once: confirmation, emails, reminder, phone call (the follow-up machine).

Budget split: most of the money goes on video one aimed cold, because reaching strangers is the only thing bought at full price. Retargeting audiences are small and cost a fraction.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- About 3% need it now, 17% gathering, 20% problem aware, 60% not thinking about it (Sabri Suby's numbers).
- Student Cheryl went from a handful of clicks to over 5,000 impressions in a couple of days, and her YouTube channel went from 5 subscribers to over 1,000.
- Cheryl's clients per year went from 8 to 14 up to 15 to 20, and her billing from $100,000 to over $250,000 a year (per Krista, student result).
- A former high school math teacher closed 3 deals before finding Krista, then 8 in year one (ads running only seven or eight months), 33 in year two, 44 in year three, 53 in year four (per Krista, student result).
- Give it 90 days of running the sequence consistently before judging it.

## Her stories and examples
- The Boost button: eleven likes, nine from people who already knew you. Lesson: right result, wrong conclusion.
- Cheryl's early start. Lesson: keep going and set campaigns up properly.
- Krista needed air conditioning work: three strangers called for bids while two people close to Krista worked in HVAC and never came to mind. Lesson: people forget even those they love without a reminder.
- The closet installer who vanished after a beautiful job. Lesson: not bad, just not visible, so no referral.
- Krista once had a problem that mattered to her and watched everything she could find about it. Lesson: people watch several videos in a row when a problem matters to them.
- The courtship analogy (meet, text, call, meals, then marriage). Lesson: do not ask a stranger for the biggest commitment first.

## What to do next
- Decide which restricted category you are in and read your licensing board's ad rules.
- Install the pixel before spending anything.
- Pick one real problem, make one video, build one single-job page.
- Start small, run video one to cold, retarget the rest, and judge only after 90 days.
- Watch early signals: watch time, and whether the retargeting pool grows week over week. Those numbers can also be shown to prospects as proof.
