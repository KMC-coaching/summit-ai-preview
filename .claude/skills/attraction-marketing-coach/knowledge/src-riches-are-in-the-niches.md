# Training: Riches Are in the Niches (Krista Mashore training)

## The one big idea
Narrowing your focus does not lose you clients. You will still attract ordinary clients, but when someone has a specific need, you are the only one who comes to mind. Krista teaches you to become the category king or queen: one group of people, one clear problem, one perfect solution (you), so people say "that is exactly me."

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **Riches are in the niches**: Her core principle that specializing brings more business, not less.
- **General practitioner versus brain surgeon**: Specialists are chosen, and paid, differently from generalists.
- **Niche**: A slice of the market for one group with one clear problem and one solution. "It's not everyone. It's someone."
- **Category king or queen**: You do not compete, you create a new lane or subcategory. You are not compared because no one else does exactly it.
- **Ownable vector**: Your niche message should be something you can own, closer to a community or industry message than a product message.
- **The 30% rule**: About one in every three videos or pieces of content speaks directly to your niche.
- **Jack of all trades, master of none / speak to everyone, speak to no one**: The risk of staying general.
- **Go deep before you go wide**: Master one niche first, then add more.
- **Spiral effect**: Being known for one thing attracts that group and others who like how you work.

## The steps or process she teaches
Choosing a niche:
1. Ask if there is a large enough market locally for your category to own (too narrow locally can work nationally).
2. Ask whether you like it, can do it, and whether it will be profitable.
3. Ask whether it solves a problem your clientele has, and one they do not know they have.
4. Identify one problem you can solve differently and what makes you unique.
5. Research competitors: is anyone already doing this?
6. Survey the market (she mentions survey tools and ChatGPT) unless you already know it will work.
Using it:
7. Make about 30% of your content speak to the niche, every week.
8. Later, with practice, make 20 to 30 videos for one niche, distribute them (website, a dedicated funnel, YouTube) so you show up in searches for that problem, then repeat for other niches.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- She says brand-new agents have gone into new markets and reached the top 1% in about 12 months, which she says she has seen repeatedly.
- Per Krista, she was a top 1% agent every year.
- She suggests one or two niches to specialize in, with a maximum of two or three.

## Her stories and examples
- She was once known as the foreclosure queen, changed her niche when the market shifted, then became the digital marketing queen. Her homes were marketed in a recognizable way and people could spot them online. She still got all kinds of clients. Lesson: a niche brand attracts the right people and does not exclude others.
- Veteran home buyer example: a generic "good time to buy" message gets ignored by a veteran who thinks they cannot qualify, but "veterans can buy with little money down" gets attention. Lesson: speak to a group about a problem they do not know they can solve.
- Industry examples she gives: an accountant for divorcing couples or farming families, at-home therapy for seniors, fitness for busy women, social media content for solo beauty pros, digital-first real estate for luxury move-up buyers relocating from high tax states, life insurance for single moms, solar for older neighborhoods, cosmetic dentistry for professionals over 40, rehab for active adults 40+, counseling for high-achieving women with anxiety and burnout, gentle chiropractic care for new moms and babies.
- Her own: marketing coach teaching business owners to grow online with video, focused on Facebook and YouTube.
- Blockbuster and Toys R Us show how an innovator can overthrow a leader, as a newcomer can with a top producer who markets poorly.

## What to do next
- Review the industry examples and break down your own profession.
- Pick one problem you solve differently, research competition, survey if needed.
- Start making niche content weekly, one in three.
