# Krista's teaching: The Monetization Multiplier

## The one big idea
Krista says your income is capped by your reach, not your talent. There are three ways to reach people (one to one, one to few, one to many), and most professionals are stuck on the first two. Your delivery may be one to one, but your marketing does not have to be, and value times reach is the equation that moves the number.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **The 3,000 households arithmetic.** If you meet one new person every working day (about 250 a year), reaching 3,000 households takes 12 years, just to meet each once.
- **One to One: The Grind.** You reaching one person, one time: networking groups, mixers, booths, coffee with a possible referral partner, and the free consultation with a price shopper. Each result costs fresh time and none carries over. It is capped by your own body and burns you out.
- **One to Few: The Referral Trap.** Past clients, patients and referral partners who send you people. Referrals are the best business, but you only stay top of mind with a couple hundred people, so you are on the stay-in-touch treadmill. Both of the first two levels make you the bottleneck.
- **One to Many.** Reaching hundreds or thousands at once with work done one time: one video, one ad, one book, one workshop, one article that AI search can hand out.
- **Activity versus asset.** An activity is something you do and it is over (a mixer, a coffee). An asset is something you own that keeps working (a video, a book, an article, a workshop recording, a guide).
- **Your work is one to one, your marketing does not have to be.** Separate how you deliver from how people find out you exist.
- **Your questions are your material.** The same twenty questions you answer every week are your one-to-many content (what it costs, how long it takes, do I need it, what if I wait, cheap version versus yours).
- **BlackBerry to Apple.** Moving from one to one to one to many is changing the game, not doing the old thing harder.
- **Innovation, differentiation, communication.** The three things Krista says Apple combined: do something genuinely different, make clear why you are not like others, and market it better than anyone.
- **The Monetization Multiplier.** Value times reach, fueled by innovation, differentiation and communication, which raises perceived value.
- **Marketing is educating.** You multiply reach by teaching something worth knowing, not by shouting your name.
- **Reach plus real value equals influence.** Influence is what makes someone choose you.

## The steps or process she teaches
Krista gives no numbered steps, but she does give a sequence of thinking:
1. Do the arithmetic on your current plan for covering your place.
2. Identify which of the three levels your business runs on.
3. Mark last month's calendar blocks as activity or asset.
4. List the questions you answer out loud every week.
5. Say each answer once, somewhere the whole zip code can listen (video, ad, book, workshop, article).
6. Keep your one-to-one delivery and let one-to-many pieces work the room before you arrive.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- 250 new people a year at one a day, so 12 years to cover 3,000 households (her arithmetic).
- Per Krista, one student's marketing was audited at over 875,000 views and impressions.
- Per Krista, Sherri built marketing around veterans with a podcast and events and got 12 deals and referrals in one stretch of a single year.
- Per Krista, she averaged over 133 homes a year for 17 years and ranked top 1% nationally for 19, and she says that was not possible one to one, so she built one to many with video, ads, a book, seminars and funnels (teaching fifty at a time).
- Per Krista, Apple is worth around three trillion dollars and BlackBerry phones are a trivia question. She also uses Netflix and Blockbuster as the same picture.
- She points to her own mindset teaching for that side.

## Her stories and examples
- **The meeting math.** Even a perfect handshake-a-day plan fails on arithmetic. Lesson: people go broke one handshake at a time on a plan that could never reach enough people.
- **Two capped professionals.** A talented hair stylist and a skilled landscaper both capped by how many people they can touch. Lesson: value was never the problem, reach was.
- **Referral slide.** Someone repeats what worked after a great year, the next year comes in softer, and they run out of people. Lesson: nothing broke, there was just no way to meet new people except by hand.
- **169 homes and invisible.** She was the busiest invisible person in her market. Lesson: the grind hid that she had built nothing that reached anyone without her.
- **BlackBerry, Netflix, Blockbuster.** Dominant players that clung to the old way lost. Lesson: while your competitors defend the keyboard phone, you can be Apple in your market.
- **One student's 875,000-view audit, then Sherri.** An unnamed student's marketing audit showed over 875,000 views and impressions (reach). Separately, Sherri built her marketing around veterans and got real business from it. Do not credit the audit to Sherri.

## What to do next
- Mark each block of your last month as activity or asset.
- Write down your twenty repeat questions.
- Record or publish an answer once, aimed at the place you picked.
- Stay on the one-to-one delivery you like, but add one-to-many pieces ahead of it.
- Plan for exposure, because one to many only works if the many see it (her teaching on getting exposure).
