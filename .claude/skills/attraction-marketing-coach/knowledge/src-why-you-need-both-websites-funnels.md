# Training: Why You Need Both a Website and a Funnel (Krista Mashore training)

## The one big idea
A website and a funnel do different jobs, and using both is the missing ingredient for most service professionals. A website is like a whole department store where you do not know why someone came in; a funnel is like a shop that sells one thing, so you know exactly what the visitor wants and can follow up specifically. If you have neither, Krista says to start with the funnel.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
**Website:** your storefront and information hub. It builds credibility, gives an overview, shows testimonials, credentials and contact details, and helps with SEO.

**Funnel:** a targeted, automated process that captures interest, nurtures and converts, built around one specific desire, pain point or outcome. It can also look just like a website.

**Lead magnet:** something of value given in exchange for contact information, and it sits inside the funnel.

**Department store versus single-purpose shop:** her analogy for why websites do not tell you what a visitor wants and funnels do.

**Jack of all trades, master of one:** speak to everyone and you speak to no one.

**Barriers to entry:** the more hurdles before someone converts, the less likely they are to. Many of her funnels give lots of information and downloads without collecting contact details.

## The steps or process she teaches
Her typical funnel path: someone who already knows, likes and trusts you goes to a page about one specific need, gets valuable information (often a lead magnet), opts in, receives an automated email or text series specific to that need, and is guided to a sales call, consultation or offer. Funnels also let you test each phase: are they clicking, what are they interested in?

## Numbers and claims she makes
- Per Krista, she has done over $70 million using sales funnels, and she often uses funnels as her website, saying she does not even look at her website.
- She says businesses using both see a 30 to 50 percent increase in lead conversion within three months, and funnel users see 20 to 30 percent better conversion rates. These are figures she states in the training without a source, so treat them as her claims.
- She says 70% of customers trust a business more if it has a website.

## Her stories and examples
- **Department store versus a shoe store.** At a department store the salesperson cannot tell why you came; at a shoe store they know you are there for shoes (and may add socks or insoles). The lesson: a funnel tells you exactly what the visitor wants.
- **Her books.** Her books tell you what to do but not how, so you still need her help. The lesson: give away lots of value; people will still need you. She also says that if you are not afraid you are giving away too much, you are not giving enough.
- **Niche examples, website versus funnel:** real estate (a free home-buying guide for a specific area), insurance (a free quote comparison), legal (free advice on a specific topic), chiropractor (a health assessment), personal trainer (a seven-day fitness challenge). The lesson: each funnel is one offer with specific follow-up.
- **People do not always opt in.** She says it is hard for students to accept that not opting in does not mean someone will not become a client; many never fill anything out and just call.

## What to do next
- Audit your website: does it say who you are, have SEO, make your address, phone and hours easy to find, and have a booking link?
- If you have neither, build a funnel first around one specific pain point or need.
- Pick a lead magnet and a follow-up series that match that one need.
