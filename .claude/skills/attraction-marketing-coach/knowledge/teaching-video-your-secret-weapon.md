# Krista's teaching: Video: Your Secret Weapon

## The one big idea
Video is the something that ads deliver and AI reads, and it is the only thing that lets a stranger experience you before you ever meet. Krista's argument: you do not need to look polished, you need to be real and to keep making videos until the awkwardness wears off. Most of the effect builds in silence, and videos do the selling long before the appointment.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **Video lets somebody experience you**: they hear your voice, see how you explain, and sense whether you would tell the truth even when it costs you the job. It is a tool for the permanent strategy of being known before you are needed.
- **Silent building**: people watch without commenting, liking or sharing, so do not judge videos by likes. Most of your real audience is invisible, and they are the ones who call.
- **"Aren't you the [what you do] person?"**: Krista's goal response. When people in your town say that to you in public, the phone call is a formality.
- **Be real, not impressive**: she says to stop performing for people who were never going to choose you. A line she credits to a student: you can't say the wrong thing to the right person.
- **Make it about the viewer**: the fix for nerves is to stop thinking about yourself and think about what the watcher fears and what you could explain in three minutes. You are explaining to a neighbor, not auditioning.
- **Volume, not perfection**: you cannot predict which video will work. There is no video gene, just practice.
- **Views While You Snooze**: video as an asset that keeps working while you sleep or live your life (links to the activity versus asset idea).
- **The 4 Steps of Video Attraction**: Hook, Position, Teach, Call to Action.
- **Short-form gets you found, long-form gets you known**: short clips pull in cold viewers; long videos bank the hours of attention that build trust.
- **Monetization Multiplier**: build once, and it banks attention repeatedly.
- **Teach the category, never the case**: for licensed or confidential fields, answer the question a hundred people have, never a specific client's private situation.
- **Video is the front door**: it gets people to notice, trust and raise a hand, but needs something waiting to catch them. It covers two letters of the FIRST Market Leader System: Found and Relevant.

## The steps or process she teaches
The 4 Steps of Video Attraction:
1. **Hook**: stop the scroll in the first sentence by naming something they already care about (stakes, fear, curiosity), not "Hi, I'm..."
2. **Position**: one line that makes you the expert without bragging (what you have seen many times).
3. **Teach**: give the real answer in plain language, and do not hold the good part back.
4. **Call to Action**: aim for engagement. Close with a question, an offer to make a video on what they want to know, or a soft direct ask. Never a pushy "call me."

Order of making videos: start with short-form for a few weeks on nothing important to get comfortable, move into long-form where trust is built, then use tools that cut the short clips out of the long recording.

Topics to point the camera at: your recurring questions, real-life questions asked at a barbecue, topics already getting views on YouTube in your space (your own take, your own town), and community content such as local partners, fundraisers or businesses.

## What to do next
- Run the competitor test: count how many of your five real competitors posted an answer to a real question this month.
- Make a few short, low-stakes clips to get used to your voice and face.
- Move into long videos answering real questions, using Hook, Position, Teach, Call to Action.
- Use an AI to find current tools that turn one long recording into short clips.
- Get your team on camera, and keep client details private.
