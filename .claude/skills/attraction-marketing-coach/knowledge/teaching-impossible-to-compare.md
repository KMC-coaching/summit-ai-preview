# Krista's teaching: You Become Impossible to Compare

## The one big idea
Write a book. Not to earn royalties but as positioning: the book is not a product, it is a position, written to sell you, not to sell books. A book makes you the authority before you say hello, takes you out of the pile of look-alike options, and so removes the price comparison that traps most local professionals. Krista says her own book is her single most powerful marketing asset.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **"She wrote the book on it"**: a figure of speech that becomes literal fact when you author the book on your problem.
- **Halo Effect**: one strong impression spills over onto everything else about you. A book is the strongest version of that stamp. Her line: people do not question authors, they hire them.
- **The book is a position, not a product**: give every copy away and you are still doing it right.
- **Borrow from outside your industry**: nobody in real estate was thinking about being an author, which is why it worked. Look for the move nobody in your field makes. Her examples: restaurants and free samples, dealerships and follow-up sequences, dentists and reminder systems.
- **The book is the questions your market already asks**: your table of contents is the dozen questions you answer constantly.
- **Title formula**: a specific problem, for a specific person, in a specific place.
- **One body of knowledge, three jackets**: one they watch (video), one an AI reads, one they hold (book).
- **Written to convert**: teach genuinely, and underneath it make the case that you are the one who gets it. Serve, not sell, printed and bound.
- **FIRST Market Leader System, the I (Impossible to Compare)**: the most permanent way out of the Commodity Trap, because competitors can copy your pricing in an afternoon and your website in a week but not the fact you wrote the book.
- **Commodity Trap**: when everyone looks the same, price becomes the only tiebreaker.
- **Whoever educates the consumer controls the consumer.**
- **Top choice equals top marketer.**

## The steps or process she teaches
How to find and write the book:
1. For two weeks, keep a note on your phone and write down every question anyone asks you, not just the smart ones.
2. You will have thirty or forty; about a dozen repeat. Those twelve are your chapters.
3. Do not write it, talk it: record yourself answering each question out loud for ten to fifteen minutes, as if the person sat across from you. About two focused afternoons.
4. Run the transcripts through AI to shape chapters in your own words.
5. Go back and fix everything that does not sound like you; the fixing makes it yours.
6. Make two versions: printed (handed across a table, sits on a counter) and digital (goes out the moment someone raises a hand online).
7. Put it under your name, with the business small underneath if you like. If there are partners, one writes it or all do.

Test of a finished chapter: would you be comfortable if someone read it, used it and never called you?

Ways the book earns its keep: it goes out before the first appointment, becomes your front door (a free copy offer, pointed at with ads), sits where your people are (front desk, waiting room, truck, welcome packet, partner business counters), feeds your content, answers the hard price question, gets you into rooms (podcasts, chamber, library, news), and wins the head-to-head.

## Her stories and examples
- A hand-off scene: the book goes home, a spouse reads it, a neighbor hears "she literally wrote the book on this, borrow mine." Lesson: one-to-many authority walks across the street without you.
- Books by profession: a dentist on braces, a remodeler on kitchen costs, an estate attorney on probate, a chiropractor on afternoon back pain, a CPA on what small businesses overpay.
- Tane, a dentist student, could write on what happens to a tooth you keep ignoring. Tim, who teaches hypnosis, could write on what it does and does not do. Lesson: no new ideas needed, only what they already say.
- Neighborhood books from her real estate days: the authority of a single block. Narrower can be a kind of person, not just a place.
- Ruth: about a year into the program she finished her book and landing page, then used the book's video to win a client.
- The price question: the author turns to the section of the book on exactly that instead of defending in the moment.

## What to do next
- Start the two-week question note today.
- Pick your twelve and record them out loud.
- Title it with a specific problem, person and place.
- Order a small print-on-demand batch and place copies where your people are.
- Use the digital copy as a free offer and point ads at it.
- If the real obstacle is the voice in your head, Krista points to her own mindset teaching.
