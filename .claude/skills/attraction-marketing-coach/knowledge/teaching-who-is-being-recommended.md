# Krista's teaching: Who Is Being Recommended, You or Your Competition

## The one big idea
People still research before they decide, but more of that research now happens inside a conversation with an AI that hands back one name instead of ten links. Krista's argument: you do not have to be the best, you have to be the clearest and most consistent source, and almost nobody in your field has done anything about it yet. The tools will change, the behavior will not.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **The recommendation test**: open an AI and ask "Who is the best [what you do] in [your city]?" in plain words. Then run four more versions: who should I hire for my exact problem, what should I know before I hire, tell me about a competitor by name, and who are the top ones and why.
- **The research phase moved**: the 17% who are gathering information used to use a search bar. Now they type whole questions, often with fear and detail, and the AI chooses for them. Google also folds AI answers into its own results.
- **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)**: making sure that when an AI is asked who to hire in your city, it has read enough of your material, in enough places, to say your name. Her contrast: SEO is about getting found when people search, AEO and GEO are about getting named when people ask.
- **The lazy AI**: the AI looks for the easiest, clearest, most verifiable answer it can feel confident about. So you win by being clearest and most consistent, not by being the best.
- **Identity Sync**: one identity everywhere, identical. Name, title, market and contact details must match across your website, Google Business Profile, directories and social profiles, down to punctuation. A confused AI moves on.
- **The three signals**: (1) one identity everywhere, (2) answering the real questions people ask, plainly and tied to your town, (3) connecting you plus what you do plus where you do it, repeated until the AI treats it as fact.
- **Two audiences**: you now write for humans and for an AI that reads all of it, so content with three human views still did its job if it taught the AI you answer that question in that town.
- **The Three Pillars**: articles that answer one exact question, video built from those questions, and regular local updates about your category.
- **Your Twelve Authority Answers**: a starter library of twelve one-topic videos (see list below).
- **FIRST Market Leader System, the R (Relevant)**: content built from the exact questions your market asks.
- **Experience does not save you, visibility does**: AI does not check your years, it checks whether you are clearly and consistently the answer.

## The steps or process she teaches
The twelve Authority Answers, in her order (each tied to your city):
1. Is now a good time to do this?
2. What does this actually cost?
3. What just changed in your category?
4. How do you choose the right person for this?
5. What are the options, and who is each right for?
6. New to your city and need what you do? Watch this first.
7. How much can you get for the budget you have?
8. A first-timer's guide.
9. How the whole process works, start to finish.
10. The biggest mistakes people make.
11. What most people in your city get wrong about your category.
12. Do you actually need this, or can it wait?

The pillar formats: an article's title is the exact question, the first sentence is the direct answer, then proof from your own town. A local update has a clear title with what you do and where, explains what changed, what it means, your take, and an invitation to talk.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (March 2026, 1,002 US adults): 6% used AI to find a local business in the 2025 edition, 45% in 2026.
- AI is the third most common way to find a local business, behind Google and Facebook and ahead of Yelp.
- 31% used ChatGPT for a recommendation in the past year; 63% of AI users trust what it gives them; 64% trust it as much as online reviews.
- Heaviest users are ages 30 to 44 (64%, versus 24% among 60 and over).
- John and Chrissy (students): over nine months, 200 blog posts, close to a thousand videos from John, a constant ad, daily social posting. No leads for several months. Then 13 leads in nine months, traced to AI tools and their website, about $1.7 million in the pipeline and roughly $32,000 coming to them (per Krista, student result).
- Another student booked a client off her YouTube channel about two and a half months in.
- Krista later says "the 82% will climb" while the survey figure given is 45%. This is an inconsistency in the text.

## Her stories and examples
- Two attorneys: one with twenty-two years and no online presence, one three years out who answers questions everywhere. The visible one gets recommended. Lesson: visibility beats experience.
- John and Chrissy at the three-month mark, defeated, who kept going. Lesson: it takes time and people quit too soon.
- Sample confession-style questions (memory care, back pain, three roof bids twelve thousand apart). Lesson: people tell AI the whole situation, so be the one who has answered it publicly.

## What to do next
- Run the recommendation test today, then the four variations, and see who comes back.
- Decide the exact version of your name, title and market and make every profile match.
- Publish answers to real questions in writing and on video, tied to your town.
- Start with the twelve Authority Answers, then add new questions as your market asks them.
- Keep going past the early silence, and run the test again in a year.
