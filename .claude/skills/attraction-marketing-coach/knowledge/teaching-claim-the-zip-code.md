# Krista's teaching: Claim the Zip Code

## The one big idea
You cannot be the obvious choice to everyone, so Krista says pick one place (or one niche) small enough to saturate and become the only name people there know. She calls this Location Domination: stacking every channel at the same people in the same place, on a schedule, for a year, until there is nothing left to compare.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **The sidewalk test.** Picture ten random people in your zip code asked to name someone who does what you do. For most readers the answer is zero.
- **Digital Location Domination.** Keep the traditional community tools (sponsoring a team, the chamber, mailers) and add a digital layer on top, so the same families see you on a banner, in the mail and in their feed.
- **Omnipresence.** Being everywhere the place lives, at once. It is the difference between being involved in a community and owning one.
- **Location Domination.** Making yourself the obvious authority of one specific place, on every channel that place lives on, at the same time.
- **Community Market Leader®.** The one name a specific place has already decided on.
- **Depth game.** Domination is about depth, not spreading across a metro an inch deep.
- **Pick the place and who lives there.** Choose an area people will actually travel to reach you, and a client type whose lifetime value is highest. Your community can be a niche instead of a zip code.
- **General answer, never the personal one.** In the mail you give market averages, ranges and common situations. A specific number for one person happens face to face.
- **The front door offer.** You cannot diagnose from a postcard, but you can make an offer on one: the smallest, cheapest, lowest-risk way to experience your work for the first time.
- **The sequence.** Planned, unsolicited value dropped into your chosen place on a schedule, for months, instead of a one-and-done.
- **Authority funnel.** Online videos and pages that each answer one real question, where watching one shows you the next and downloading a guide triggers a video.
- **Serve, not sell.** The more you serve, the more you sell, and the more a place serves, the more it sells for you.
- **EPIC.** Her framework for the first appointment: Position, Impact, Conversion. The letters are **E**quity, **P**resentation, **I**mpact, **C**ampaign.
- **The FIRST Market Leader System.** Found, Impossible to Compare, Relevant, Scaled, Top Choice.

## The steps or process she teaches
Picking and owning a place:
1. Pick the place: an area people will travel to reach you (the whole town in a small market, a few zip codes in a dense metro).
2. Check it against your numbers: how many new clients you need a year, and how many households or businesses sit inside. If a small slice choosing you covers your year, it is big enough, so go deeper not wider.
3. Pick who lives there: the people whose lifetime value to you is highest.
4. Give the general answer in the mail and online, and save the specific answer for the conversation.
5. Find a partner business that serves the same people and takes none of your money, and split the cost.
6. Run it as a sequence, not a one-off.

Her six-touch sequence, about four weeks apart, each giving something and asking for nothing: (1) the general answer to the question your future client is asking, (2) a card with a QR code into something useful for their area, (3) a real guide with your face on page one, (4) a piece that gently unsettles what they read online (written for someone elsewhere), (5) something seasonal, (6) a clean no-pressure offer to talk.

When someone raises a hand: call to set the appointment and get something useful into their hands first. Suggest they ask the other people they are considering whether any of them sent anything before the meeting.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- Per Krista, her own plan: two neighborhoods, a postcard every two weeks (new listings, pending, sold, local coupons and team schedules on the back), shared cost with a lender friend, plus fun events. This got her back over a hundred homes a year.
- Per Krista, a student went from no Google presence to over five million views inside a year by reviewing and filming local businesses.
- Per Krista, Heather had her best year ever during the worst year her market had seen in three decades, and she doubled.
- Advertising caution per Krista: real estate ads fall under housing rules that widen the radius, so she built a custom audience from a title company list. Most local service businesses are not under housing rules, but lending or credit businesses have restrictions, so check your own category first.
- A year is the commitment: do not choose a place unless you will show up in it for a full year.

## Her stories and examples
- **Heather.** Strangers call her to hire her because she is everywhere in the streets she chose. Lesson: pick a place and refuse to be anything other than its obvious name.
- **Debbie the lender.** Split the cost of the postcards with someone who served the same families. Lesson: find your version of Debbie (dentist and orthodontist, CPA and estate attorney).
- **Seven students, seven niches.** Coaches, small business owners, funnel builders, tapping, hypnosis, yoga and wellness, and a dentist. Lesson: nobody tried to be for everybody.
- **Tane the dentist, Stacy, Raquel.** Each got a six-beat sequence of videos, guides and an offer. Lesson: the shape is the same and only the contents change.
- **Divorce attorney, contractor, advisor.** Each opens with the person's position and what waiting costs, not a price. Lesson: that is Equity.

## What to do next
- Pick your place (or niche) and your ideal client inside it.
- Decide your front-door offer.
- Plan a six-touch sequence and keep it going for a year.
- Stack channels on the same people (mail, video, funnel, events, reviews of local businesses).
- Prepare the first appointment around Equity, Presentation, Impact and Campaign.
