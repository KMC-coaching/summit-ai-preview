# Krista's teaching: Set the Goal, Burn the Boats

## The one big idea
The systems she teaches only work if a person actually runs them, and whether you run them, and keep running them when it is slow and uncomfortable, is your decision. Start with the vision (the life the number buys), turn it into written goals, put the work on the calendar, make a real decision (a line in the sand), and get support so you stay decided. Be known or go broke is just arithmetic.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **Vision before the number**: the picture of the life a number buys. Krista names four common themes: time, security, identity and legacy. "The number is just the odometer."
- **The thirty-minute vision process**: pick categories (hers: Marriage, Family, Business, Personal, Giving Back), brainstorm each for five minutes, mark each item as a one-, two- or three-year thing, then write two minutes on why the top one-year item matters.
- **Aim goal vs. end goal**: the vision is an aim goal (a direction that never ends); an end goal is one specific result, a stepping stone to the vision.
- **SMART, expanded version**: Specific and simple; Measurable and meaningful; Achievable, all areas of your life, and as if now (present tense, "I am"); Realistic and responsible; Timed and toward what you want (stated positively).
- **Fantasy, goal, action**: a dream without written goals is a fantasy; a goal without action is fiction. The calendar test: if the work is not in blocks on your calendar, the goal is not important yet.
- **The ten traits**: commitment over convenience; courage over fear and faith over fear; draw a line in the sand, decide, burn the boats; resources versus resourceful (need both); ask who, not how; R.O.I. or C.O.I. (return on investment or cost of inaction); cost versus investment; ask for support, not permission; persistence plus consistency; let go of shame and self-doubt. Krista says every one is a choice, not talent.
- **Wanting vs. deciding**: wanting is a feeling; deciding is a line in the sand and a filter you run choices through.
- **Burn the boats**: remove retreat so quitting is off the table; a plan B distracts from plan A.
- **Cost of inaction**: put a conservative number on a client's lifetime value, add one or two extra clients a month, multiply by a year; nothing compounds in a year you did not build.
- **The FIRST Market Leader System recap**: Found, Impossible to Compare, Relevant, Scaled, Top Choice. Also Community Market Leader.

## The steps or process she teaches
The first ninety days:
1. Week one: decide the place (one zip code, set of neighborhoods or kind of person).
2. Week one: do the thirty-minute vision process and turn one piece into a single SMART end goal; put it on the mirror.
3. Week one: block two hours, twice a week, for twelve weeks on the calendar.
4. Week one: write the twenty questions you already answer every week (your content plan).
5. Week two: record the first video (one question, ninety seconds, on your phone).
6. Week two: build the one page (one job, one giveaway, one place to raise a hand).
7. Week three: put ads behind it, aimed at your chosen place.
8. Week four: decide your front door, the smallest, cheapest, lowest-risk first experience, priced so yes is easy.
9. Repeat for eleven more weeks; do not stop at ninety days. Ask who, not how, for the thing you will avoid.

Write goals where you see them daily, read them morning and night; Krista records hers and makes vision boards.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- Her own coaching experience: thousands coached (per Krista).
- The British men's eight rowing team went 88 years without Olympic gold and won in 2000 using the question "Will it make the boat go faster?" (Ben Hunt-Davis and Harriet Beveridge, 2011).
- "Change or Die" (Alan Deutschman, Fast Company, 2005): about 90% of heart patients reverted within a year; a group in regular group support sustained the change at about 77% three years later. "Seven times more likely" is Krista's own framing.
- The expanded SMART and aim/end goal ideas come from Dr. Matthew James.
- The three-feet-from-gold story is from Napoleon Hill, Think and Grow Rich.
- She sold 169 homes in her best year, then dropped to about 12 when the market turned (per Krista).

## What to do next
Decide today, before the feeling wears off. Write your vision and goals, pick your place, put blocks on the calendar, surround yourself with people going where you are going, make the first small move and then the next. Krista points to her own mindset teaching for more on deciding.
