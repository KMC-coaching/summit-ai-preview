# Krista's teaching: Stop Renting Your Business

## The one big idea
Most people use AI as a renter: they teach it their business every day, it forgets, and they own nothing they built. Krista's argument is to build on a system you own, where every correction is saved once and compounds, and to hand that system the work that frees you up while never handing off the relationship, your face, the hard conversations, or your judgment.

## Named ideas, frameworks and terms
- **You're paying somebody's mortgage**: Krista's old pitch to renters at the kitchen table (a stack of receipts, an unstable landlord). She says it back to the reader about AI.
- **Every conversation with an AI is rent**: you paid, explained your business and got better, but you own no file. The better a tenant you get, the richer the landlord gets.
- **The forgetful assistant**: imagine hiring someone who forgets everything each morning. You would let them go in a week, yet that is most people's AI setup.
- **The Greenhouse**: renting AI means growing your business in someone else's greenhouse, where they set the rent and can lock the door. In a greenhouse you own, scraps and corrections become compost. Her line: rented work disappears, owned work compounds.
- **Owning on a Tuesday, three things**: (1) your business has already reported to you (a morning brief, like a hot sheet for your business: who is drifting, which job is stalled, whose anniversary is Thursday, what you promised and did not do), (2) every correction belongs to you (teach it once and it holds), (3) it is quiet.
- **Silence on success. Speak on exception.**: the rule her system runs on. It says nothing when all is fine, and speaks when something broke, needs your decision, or is about to cost money.
- **Three questions in ten seconds**: what is coming in the next ninety days, who has gone quiet who should not have, and where did my last twenty clients actually come from. If you cannot answer, it is a missing system, not a discipline problem.
- **Highest and best use**: ask what only you can do, what someone with less training could do, and what drains you. The last two are your delegation list.
- **The third answer**: besides doing it yourself or hiring a person, you can hand it to an agent built for one job that already knows your business because you trained it.
- **The Authority Operating System**: the version Krista built for herself and teaches, a team of separate agents (one drafts, one tracks what got done versus promised, one watches the whole business).
- **Owning never meant understanding the machine**: like a car or a home, ownership means holding the title.
- **The Line You Do Not Cross**: never hand off the relationship, your face on video, the hard client conversation, or the judgment and advice.

## The steps or process she teaches
Krista gives no numbered procedure. It gives a standard to test any AI setup against: Does it remember? Does the correction stay corrected? Do the files live somewhere with your name on them? Can anybody change the terms on you tonight? If yes to the last, you are renting.

## Numbers and claims she makes
- Her renter math: two thousand a month for five years is a hundred and twenty thousand dollars with nothing to show (per Krista, illustrative).
- Krista says she is not a programmer and has never written code. She built her system by talking to it and correcting it, as with a new hire (per Krista).
- Her listing example: up to 26 offers on one house. Homes in that market came in under appraisal three or four times a month. She vetted buyers' agents and lenders herself, and one house still came in eighteen thousand dollars under, but the seller got the full contract price because the offer had protection built in (per Krista).
- No outside statistics are cited.

## Her stories and examples
- Kitchen tables with renters: the line alone never closed anyone, the math did. Lesson: the pitch works because every word is true, and it applies to AI renting.
- The 26-offer listing and the phone calls she made to vet buyers. Lesson: a system can tell you the call is worth making, but cannot make it or hear what you hear in someone's voice.
- Early years at the beck and call of every client, then setting limits (not Sundays, not after seven). Lesson: people respected her more, and boundaries protect your best work.
- The car and the home as things you own without understanding. Lesson: title, not technical skill.

## What to do next
- Ask honestly what you own from your AI use so far.
- Put your voice, market, prices and corrections into files that live with your name on them.
- Test the three ten-second questions on your own business.
- List tasks that are not your highest and best use and consider handing them to a trained agent.
- Delegate what frees you to be present with clients, and keep the relationship, face, hard conversations and judgment.
- Build the owned system first, before making the videos, funnel, class and book that follow.
